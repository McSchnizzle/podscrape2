# AI and Technology Digest Instructions

## Topic Focus
Focus on artificial intelligence developments, machine learning breakthroughs, AI product launches, technology industry news, tech company developments, new product releases, tech culture discussions, digital trends, and tech policy that shape the broader technology landscape.

## Episode Coverage (~80% of the script)
- Open cold on the single most striking, concrete detail from today's sources: a number, a startling claim, a quote. No welcome, no date, no lineup until the hook has landed. Weave the welcome in a few turns later, loose and dated ("Welcome to the digest, July ninth. I'm Amara. Four episodes today...").
- Cover roughly four source stories in depth rather than many shallowly. Stay on one story and actually follow its argument (roughly seven to nine thousand characters each) before moving on.
- Cite sources as specific episodes, hosts, and reporters: "The Vergecast this week had reporter Liz Lopatto on to cover...", "covered in the episode 'Where the Economy Thrives After AI'". The digest is openly a digest of other podcasts.
- Weave cross-references between stories naturally into the coverage ("which loops back to the argument from..."), never as a separate section.
- Transitions are conversational and evocative, never numbered or labeled: "Okay. Now let's go somewhere with very different energy."
- Cover technical, business, policy, and culture angles organically inside each story as the material calls for it, not as separate category passes.
- Spell numbers TTS-friendly: "a hundred and twelve billion dollars", "twenty-seven percent", "five hundred twelve gigabytes"; never raw symbols or digits like "$47.45" or "512 GB".

## Connecting the Threads (2-3 exchanges)
After the stories, a short exchange naming the through-line of the day: what these stories have to do with each other, in plain language. This is synthesis, not summary. Do not restate facts already covered.

## Closing: the detail that stays (2-3 exchanges)
- Each host names the one detail from today's sources they'll keep thinking about, and why. Specific, not thematic.
- Then a short, warm, dated sign-off with host names on air: "July ninth. Back tomorrow." / "Thanks for listening. Take care." Two lines, no more.

## What NOT to Do
- Do NOT speak section scaffolding aloud: no "Key Highlights", no "Detailed Analysis", no numbered highlights ("our second highlight"), no category buckets ("AI Model Releases and Updates:"), no "Actionable takeaways", no "Risks to monitor", no "What to watch next".
- Do NOT pad citations with outlet boilerplate ("TechCrunch and MIT Technology Review have both stressed..."). Cite the actual source episode or a specific named report, or say nothing.
- Do NOT restate data already covered anywhere in the script. Each fact appears once.
- Do NOT segment advice by audience ("for developers...", "for leaders...").
- Do NOT close with an essay conclusion or a credits roll. No "Final word", no "Credit where it's due".
- Do NOT put an audio tag on every turn. Most turns carry no tag; tags mark genuine reactions and tonal shifts only.
